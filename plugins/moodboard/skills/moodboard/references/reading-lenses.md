# Reading a reference

How to look at one saved image and extract more than a palette. Use whenever a
specific reference is driving decisions: always at strict level, and at guided level
for the references that end up as drivers. Ambient work skips this - the taste profile
already did the reading.

The motion is two-step: look through a lens, then end the look with a transferable
principle - one sentence that survives leaving the image. "Oil on canvas, 1962" is
trivia; "the medium forces slow blended transitions, so nothing snaps" is a principle
you can build with.

## Lenses

Each lens is a question plus what it yields. Not every lens fires on every image -
3-4 strong reads beat 8 forced ones. Skip a lens rather than pad it.

1. **Form** - What are the dominant lines, shapes, values, scale relationships?
   -> geometry, contrast curve, visual weight distribution.
2. **Material** - What is it made of, and what did the material's constraints force?
   Oil blends where watercolor bleeds; concrete is monolithic where linen drapes.
   -> surface treatment, texture, how much imperfection to allow.
3. **Process** - How was it made: hand or machine, additive or subtractive, fast
   gesture or slow accumulation? The making always leaves marks.
   -> edge quality, repetition style, tolerance for irregularity.
4. **Era and lineage** - When, where, which movement, by whom - and what was it
   reacting against? Most strong work is an argument with something.
   -> type direction, cultural register, and half the anti-patterns.
5. **Composition** - How is the eye moved? Where is the emptiness and what is it
   doing? -> layout, hierarchy, spacing, grid vs drift.
6. **Function** - What job did this originally do (garment, chair, poster, shrine)?
   -> find the equivalent job in the target before copying the look.
7. **Mood** - Emotional temperature: austere, tender, aggressive, serene.
   -> motion feel, copy tone, how hard the contrast hits.
8. **Refusals** - What does it conspicuously not do? -> anti-patterns, derived
   rather than stock.

For moving references (gifs, video stills): process and mood extend over time -
read pacing, easing, and cut rhythm as part of lenses 3 and 7.

## Application moves

One image fans into many applications because each extracted attribute can transfer
at a chosen level of literalness:

- **Quote** - lift it directly: the hex, the exact grid ratio. Highest fidelity;
  use sparingly or the result is a costume.
- **Translate** - carry the attribute across mediums: drape becomes overlapping
  layout; woodblock registration error becomes slightly offset color layers.
- **Abstract** - keep only the principle ("one material does all the work",
  "worn beats pristine") and re-derive the execution natively in the target.
- **Invert** - use the reference as a foil: do the opposite on purpose, and say so
  in the brief.

A strong application mixes moves: quote the palette, translate the composition,
abstract the process, keep the refusals. The same image applied twice should not
produce the same design - the mix of moves is where the range lives.

## Worked example

One photo - a Raf Simons 2003 bomber, lone figure against an empty field - applied
to a landing page:

- Form: single high-contrast figure vs empty ground -> one hero element, huge
  negative space, nothing competing.
- Material: military nylon with sewn-on patches, worn -> flat matte surfaces with
  one deliberate collage moment.
- Process: surplus garments recontextualized, not made from scratch -> standard
  components restyled off-registration rather than custom everything.
- Era: post-punk Antwerp, anti-fashion -> brutalist mono type, refuse friendly
  SaaS chrome.
- Mood: alienated tenderness -> restrained motion, quiet copy.

The same image feeding a deck would instead quote its photographic register; feeding
a physical product, its material honesty. Different targets, different mixes, one
reference.
