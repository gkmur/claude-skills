## Corpus taste profile

Across every design-relevant board, one sensibility repeats: high-contrast monochrome as
the default, color used as a single deliberate accent rather than a palette. Black,
white, cream/paper, and charcoal carry most compositions (#000000-#2a2a29 through
#f5f5f5-#fafafa), with warm neutrals (wood tan #b69c7c, #9c8977, leather #616b6d) doing
the "color" work in interiors and fashion, and cool accent hues appearing rarely and on
purpose (crimson #d52508, #7f2726; cyan/teal #3a5b84, #235754). Avoid full-saturation
multi-color palettes; when color shows up it should read like a single ink, not a
scheme.

Materials skew tactile and honest: raw leather, washed/worn denim, natural linen, oak
and ash wood, concrete, brushed steel, frosted/etched glass. Distressing, fraying, and
patina are treated as features, not flaws — worn > pristine. Reject anything glossy-
plastic, corporate-stock, or synthetic-looking unless it's explicitly glassmorphic UI
(where translucency itself is the material).

Composition habits: generous negative space, strict grid or centered symmetry,
high-contrast figure-ground relationships (a stark subject against a near-empty field).
Halftone/dot-matrix printing, ink-wash brushwork, and stippled or grainy textures recur
as surface treatments even in digital pieces — the eye favors printed-paper texture over
clean vector flatness. Abstract work leans geometric-Swiss (overlapping circles, grids,
constructivist marks) or organic-biomorphic (soft blobs, concentric curves) — rarely
both mixed in one piece.

Photographic/lighting mood: documentary and contact-sheet framing, grainy film stock,
chiaroscuro and hard directional light for fashion/editorial; soft diffused warm light
(paper/linen pendant lamps, golden hour) for interiors and lifestyle. Mood words that
recur unprompted across captions: austere, contemplative, serene, brutalist, avant-
garde, edgy. Whimsical/playful only shows up in throwaway boards (Lol, some anime) —
it is not part of the serious taste signature.

Era and subculture signals: 1990s-2000s Antwerp/Japanese deconstructionist fashion (Raf
Simons, Helmut Lang, Comme des Garcons, Yohji Yamamoto, Margiela) is the strongest single
through-line in the whole corpus — brutalist typography, grayscale archive photography,
oversized/deconstructed silhouettes. Adjacent to that: mid-century modernist furniture
(Herman Miller, Noguchi, Eames), Japandi minimalism, and Swiss/constructivist graphic
design. Apple's product-design language (soft gradients, glass, extreme restraint)
is a recurring reference specifically for UI/UX and product work, kept separate from
the grittier fashion/editorial register.

Typography sensibility implied throughout: bold sans-serif set small against huge
whitespace, or oversized/overlapping serif-adjacent display type on stark grounds
(book covers, brand lockups like "HELMUT LANG", "1991 COMME des GARCONS"). Type is
used as a graphic block, not decoration — high contrast, minimal color, generous
kerning-driven drama over ornamentation.

Anti-patterns — what this taste consistently rejects: cheerful multi-color palettes,
rounded-friendly "startup" illustration style, drop-shadow skeuomorphism outside of
intentional glassmorphism, cluttered maximalist layouts, cutesy/kawaii aesthetics
(present only in the low-signal anime/lol boards, never in the design-serious ones),
stock-photo gloss, and anything that reads as "safe corporate template." Novelty
color-blocking (Y2K jewel tones, neon gradients) appears only as an occasional
counterpoint pin, never as a load-bearing direction — treat it as an accent option,
not a base palette.

## Boards

### portfolio inspo — use for: graphic design/branding/poster reference, UI/product design
Personal design-reference dump: Apple product/UI aesthetics, Helmut Lang and Japanese
avant-garde fashion branding, Swiss modernist poster abstraction (overlapping circles,
halftone portraits), Louise Bourgeois-scale sculpture. Dominant palette: stark black/
white/cream (#020202, #fafbfc range) with occasional soft pastel glass gradients
(#f4f4f4, iridescent pink-cyan). Use for: any brand identity, poster, or app-UI work
that needs restraint plus one moment of drama.

### editorials — use for: fashion/campaign art direction, moody lookbook layouts
Archive fashion editorial: Raf Simons, Carol Christian Poell, Undercover, Number(N)ine —
early-2000s Antwerp/Japanese deconstructionism, grunge-meets-runway, forest and
industrial-concrete settings. Palette: dark forest greens (#36442e, #32482c), charcoal
and near-black (#292929, #1c0d22), one hot red accent (#d52508). Use for: lookbooks,
campaign mood, any brief calling for "cool, brooding, avant-garde menswear."

### fashion — use for: garment/product photography styling, streetwear/techwear detail shots
Product-level fashion reference: cropped jackets, distressed leather, denim, eyewear
(Gentle Monster), keychains/hardware. Palette: black leather and denim (#1b1b1b,
#272623), warm skin/wood neutrals (#b69c7c, #ac8c62). Use for: close-up material and
hardware detail shots, texture references for apparel or accessory design.

### home — use for: interior styling, product/furniture photography, Japandi spaces
Warm Japandi and mid-century interiors: Herman Miller/Eames/Noguchi furniture, MUJI
storage, wood-and-linen bathrooms, cat furniture. Palette: honey wood tones (#ba7a43,
#9f8d77, #aa8161), soft neutral white-gray (#e1e0de, #ebebeb). Use for: any lifestyle,
real-estate, or product-in-context shot needing calm, natural-material warmth.

### interior design — use for: workspace/studio environment references
Studio and home-office spaces: brutalist concrete-and-wood workspaces, minimalist
bedrooms, USM/Haller furniture. Palette: cool gray-brown neutrals (#766f69, #928d87,
concrete tones). Use for: "creative studio" or "focused workspace" environment
backgrounds in decks or product shots.

### prints — use for: wall-art / poster asset selection, illustration mood reference

Palette: cream/kraft paper with black ink (#ede2d0, #c6beae), occasional jewel-tone

commissions, anything wanting a Japanese-print register.

### objects — use for: abstract background textures, generative-art accents

warm red mesh (#d1b7b0). Use for: subtle abstract background texture behind text-heavy
