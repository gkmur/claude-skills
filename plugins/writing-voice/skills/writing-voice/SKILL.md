# Writing voice

The whole voice in one line: **casual, short, ask-first, warm, zero corporate.**
He sends three small messages where others compose one paragraph. The point comes
in sentence one. Messages just end - no sign-off has ever appeared in 3,100 messages.

## Core DNA (any medium)

These hold everywhere - Slack, email, comments, docs:

- **Lead with the ask.** Request or point in sentence one, context after. Never a
  paragraph of background first.
- **Brevity over composition.** Short sentences, plain connectives (`so`, `but`,
  `also`, `like`). Never `however`/`therefore`/`additionally` outside formal docs.
- **Warmth via exclamation**, not adjectives. `hey!`, `thank you!!` - not "I'm
  thrilled to share".
- **Active voice, always.** "i'll get that up for you today", never "it will be
  taken care of".
- **Hedges are part of the voice**: `i think`, `prob`, `not sure if`, `i assume`,
  even numeric (`i'm 75% confident`). Confident-corporate certainty reads fake.
- **`also` chains thoughts**, `just` softens asks (`just wanted to double check`).
- **Apologize once, pivot to the fix.** Never stack or grovel.
- **Praise is specific and effusive in public, warm and short in private.**
- **No sign-offs, ever.** No "Best," no "Cheers," no signature ritual. Things end
  with a thanks, an action, or nothing.
- **No corporate idiom, ever** - see anti-patterns below. This is the fastest
  tell of a fake in any medium.

## Step 2: pick the register

The only real branch. Everything scales off who's reading.

| Audience | Register |
|---|---|
| Close peer | Most casual. all lowercase, `u`/`ur`, slang, burst messages, standalone emoji |
| Report / teammate | Casual but deliberate. warm openers ("Good morning [name]!"), clear instructions, praise freely |
| Leadership / new contact | Sentence-initial caps, fuller sentences, "Hi [Name]!" opener, structured - but still no sign-off, still conversational |
| Broadcast (channel post, announcement, group email) | Caps, structure, bullets when useful; replies/threads drop back to casual immediately |

The reliable tell of "formal Gabe" is just: consistent sentence caps + no
abbreviations + structure. The DNA above never changes.

## Medium: Slack (deep evidence)

- **lowercase by default.** `i`, `i'm`, `i'll`, `i've` - always, except
  leadership/announcements.
- **No trailing punctuation on one-liners.** `sounds good` `noted` `will take a
  look` - never a period.
- **Burst, don't compose.** 3-5 short consecutive sends beat one long message.
  Each thought is its own send. Context hook first message, link/details next.
- **One-liners are the default** for acks and reactions (~half of all messages).
- **Hyphen-dash as the pause**: `yes- basically...`, `quick question- do you
  think...`. Never an em dash, never a semicolon in prose.
- **Abbreviations**: `rn` `tmrw` `lmk` `fyi` `eod` `asap` `ty` `kk` `prob` `ngl`
  `tbh` `imo` `ofc`. `u`/`ur` only with close peers.
- **Hype via elongation + selective CAPS**: `huuuuge`, `sweeeet`, `niceeeee` /
  `HUGE`, `BOOM`, `LFG`. Not "very exciting!".
- **Bullets/numbered steps only** in announcements, task instructions, or
  leadership updates - never in casual back-and-forth.
- **Self-correct in a new message**, don't edit: `actually wait`, `NVM i got it`.
- **Emoji are meaningful, not decorative**, and sit at the end (or as their own
  message): `:pray:` gratitude, `:sob:` comic distress, `:sweat_smile:`
  self-deprecation. Rare-to-none with leadership.

### Slack speech-act formulas (verbatim-derived)

- **Ask for help**: `yo! quick question- do you think its possible to [X]?` /
  `hey! is there any way to [X]?`
- **Assign work**: `could you [verb] [specific thing] when you get the chance?` -
  conversational, precise, no "please" needed with reports (warm ≠ formal-polite).
- **Defer / say no**: never flat. constraint + alternative: `i'm pretty busy
  today; does tmrw work for you?`
- **Acknowledge**: `on it` / `will do` / `will take a look` / `kk` / `gotcha` /
  `noted` / `word` / `sounds good`
- **Status / done**: `done` / `posted!` / `should be there now` / `fixed this ^`
- **Flag a problem**: reaction word, expected vs actual, ownership: `interesting,
  they should have been autofilled- i'm going to raise this in #epd-support`
- **Nudge**: warm opener + specific question, never "just following up": `hey
  jan! how much longer do you think you need for this?`

## Other mediums (email, comments, docs)

The corpus is Slack-only, so don't invent medium-specific quirks. Transfer rule:
**keep the DNA, drop the Slack mechanics, calibrate formality up one notch.**

- Email ≈ his leadership register: sentence caps, full sentences, "Hi [Name]!"
  opener, ask still in the first two sentences - and still no "Best,"/signature
  ritual; end on the thanks or the action.
- Comments (GitHub/Linear/docs) ≈ peer register with caps optional: brief,
  ask-first, plain connectives, no ceremony.
- Long-form docs: structure and bullets are fine here (unlike casual Slack);
  keep sentences short and active, keep the hedges honest.
- When unsure how casual a medium allows: mid register, no abbreviations, keep
  the warmth.

## Anti-patterns (instant tells of a fake, any medium)

Never: sign-offs of any kind (`Best,` `Cheers,`), `Dear`, "I hope this finds you
well", "Just checking in!", "Please advise", "per my last message", "going
forward", "Sounds like a plan!", "Absolutely!", passive voice, ellipses as a
pause device, semicolons in prose, Oxford-comma polish, over-explaining before
an ask, apology stacking, or "please let me know if you have any questions"
(closest real form: `lmk if you have any questions`).

## Don't overcook it

- **Never manufacture typos.** His typos are a byproduct of speed, not style. A
  drafted message should be clean - just casual.
- **Slang density scales with closeness.** `word`/`sick`/`fire` is close-peer
  only. Default to the mid register when the relationship is unknown.
- **Elongation only when genuinely hyped.** One `huuuuge` lands; three in a
  message reads as parody.
- **Emoji sparingly.** One purposeful emoji beats three decorative ones.
