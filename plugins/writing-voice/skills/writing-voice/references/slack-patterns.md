# Slack voice patterns — full evidence profile

Provenance: distilled from ~3,100 verbatim messages Gabe sent on Slack between
Sept 2022 and June 2026, collected across all channels/DMs in 7 time slices and
analyzed independently for the early era (2022–2024H1) and recent era
(2024H2–2026). Recent era wins wherever the two disagree — it's his current voice.
Quotes below are verbatim except names, which are trimmed to first names.

Frequency scale: always / usually / sometimes / rare.

## 1. Capitalization & spelling

- **lowercase `i`, `i'm`, `i'll`, `i've` — always** in DMs and casual channel
  messages, across the entire 4-year corpus. The single most reliable pattern.
  - "i think it worked" · "i'll take a look rn" · "i'm no coder so if you think it can be improved let me know"
- **Sentence-initial caps — only** in (a) channel announcements, (b) messages to
  leadership or brand-new contacts, (c) deliberate/serious moments.
  - "Hi Daniele, the time works just fine! Excited to meet!" · "Happy Tuesday team! Thank you all for your summaries"
- Trend: the lowercase default *strengthened* over time; by 2025–26 caps are
  almost exclusively a leadership/announcement marker.
- **Abbreviations (usually):** `rn`, `tmrw`, `lmk`, `fyi`, `eod`, `eow`, `asap`,
  `ty`, `kk`, `prob`, `ngl`, `tbh`, `tbf`, `imo`, `ofc`, `atp`, `yk`, `nvm`, `jk`.
- **`u` / `ur` (sometimes, close peers only):** "lmk if u got any suggestions" ·
  "ur the goat" · "did u fill out the 3 fields at the bottom"
- **Typos stay (sometimes).** In fast back-and-forth he sends and moves on
  ("shouldb e goodn ow", "yup it owrks", "i'll check rightn ow"); occasionally
  patches with an asterisk follow-up ("succeed*"). Imitators should NOT inject
  typos — they're speed artifacts, not style.

## 2. Punctuation

- **One-liners end with nothing — always.** "correct" · "noted" · "yup" ·
  "sounds good" · "kk ty"
- **Periods — sometimes**, only inside/at the end of multi-sentence explanations.
- **Exclamation marks — very common.** Warmth markers on openers, thanks,
  completions, praise. Stacking `!!`/`!!!` scales with excitement:
  "Amazing work Lynn!!" · "thank you!!!!!"
- **Question marks — always** on real questions. "does that help? or am i still
  missing your point"
- **Hyphen-dash as pause/connector (signature):** "yes- basically was just
  working with Navid" · "quick question- do you think its possible to add..." ·
  "I'm doing well- hope you are too."
- **Semicolons — rare** (recent era only, joining tight clauses: "quick update;
  i'm working on centralizing the metrics for you right now."). **Em dashes,
  ellipses — never** in natural prose.
- **Parentheses — sometimes**, for genuinely supplementary info:
  "(about ~4.5-5k a month cut)" · "(it will give you options to choose from)"
- Commas are conversational and sometimes missing where formal English wants them.

## 3. Message shape

- **One-liners are ~40–50% of all messages.** Single words ("done", "word",
  "sick", "fire", "agreed", "roger") up through five words.
- **Burst sequencing (signature, frequent):** several short consecutive sends
  instead of one composed message — think-as-you-go. Context hook first, then
  the link/details as the next message.
  - "it runs but doesn't seem to place any urls in the image column" → "i sorted the search key too so not sure whats goin on" → "kk" → "ty"
  - "will send it over in a sec" → "[link]"
- **Front-load the ask — always.** "hey! in a meeting right now- could you do
  sometime around 2:35?" Context follows the point, never precedes it. (To
  leadership, slightly more context, but the point still comes early.)
- **Bullets / numbered steps — sometimes**, strictly for channel announcements,
  task instructions, and structured leadership updates ("here is the order of
  operations; 1. try and fill in the brand..."). Never in casual DMs.
- **Self-corrections happen as new messages**, not edits: "actually wait" ·
  "NVM i got it" · "oops" · "jk".

## 4. Openers & closers

Openers:
- **Close peers:** "yo" / "yo!" / "yoyo", or no greeting at all.
  "yo! quick question- ..."
- **Peers / general DMs:** "hey!" is the default unit (with the !).
  "hey! hope you had a good start to the day. quick question..."
- **Reports, start of day:** "Good morning [Name]!" / "Morning!"
- **Leadership / new contacts:** "Hi [Name]!" — one line, then straight to it.
  "Hi Erik! I'm doing well, hope you're good too."
- **Mid-thread — usually no opener at all.** Picks up exactly where it left off.
- Channels: dives straight in, or `<!here>` / @mention when calling attention;
  "Good morning team!" for morale posts.

Closers:
- **No formal sign-off — never, in 3,100 messages.** No "Best," no "Cheers,"
  no name. Messages just end.
- When something closes a message: "ty" / "tyty" / "preesh" / "appreciate it" /
  "thank you!" / "lmk" / "lets go" / "LFG" / a single purposeful emoji.
- To reports at end of day: "have a great day/night [Name]!" (established 2023+).
- To leadership: ends on a period or "Thank you!" — still no sign-off.

## 5. Emoji

- In-text emoji are **purposeful, end-of-message, moderate frequency**; whole
  messages that are just one emoji are common with peers.
- Meanings: `:pray:` gratitude/relief · `:sob:` comic distress or overwhelmed
  thanks ("tysm :sob:") · `:sweat_smile:` self-deprecation · `:pensive:`
  resignation · `:+1:` plain ack · `:heart:`/`:heart_hands:` team appreciation ·
  custom hype emoji (party parrot, cool-doge, etc.) for wins.
- Density: highest in team/peer contexts, near zero with leadership or in
  formal support posts.

## 6. Speech acts (verbatim formulas)

- **Ask for help:** "hey! is there any way to get our api key limits reset or
  extended?" · "yo! quick question- do you think its possible to add the date
  the item was posted?" — ask immediately, minimal preamble; "quick question"
  is a recurring pre-amble.
- **Assign work:** "could you transpose the lot in this file when you get the
  chance?" · "can you go through here and fill in the brands based off the
  inventory file link?" · "once you're done transposing those two lots, move to
  awaiting itemization and tag me please." — conversational verb-first request +
  precise object; "please" rare and only as a softener tail, never formal-polite.
- **Defer / say no:** constraint + alternative, almost never flat. "i'm pretty
  busy today; does tmrw work for you?" · "Hey man would love to but I'm
  currently cramming on some stuff for Jen rn; can you give me some idea of the
  help you need?" · "Can't atm, im in a meeting with product right now". Flat
  version exists but is rare: "I won't be attending George" — clean, no apology.
- **Acknowledge:** "on it!" · "will do" · "will take a look" · "looking into
  this now!" · "kk" · "gotcha" · "noted" · "word" · "roger" · "sounds good"
- **Status / done:** "done" · "posted!" · "its all done" · "should be there
  now" · "fixed this ^" · "[link]" then "ready for review!" · "images are done!
  id say its around 85% accurate so double check" — short, sometimes
  celebratory, link travels in its own message.
- **Apologize:** once, brief, then the fix or pivot. "sorry for the late ping" ·
  "my bad i got side tracked" · "that was my fault!! sorry about that" ·
  "Sorry about that had a bug in the automation but it's good now". Never
  stacked, never groveling.
- **Praise:** public = effusive + specific ("Huge shoutout to one of our new
  itemizers..." · "GO Lynn!!!!" · "you're gonna KILL this!!! super excited for
  you"); private = warm + short ("sweeeet nice job" · "you're the best" ·
  "life saver" · "We love a resourceful queen").
- **Flag a problem:** reaction word → expected vs actual → ownership of next
  step. "interesting, they should have been autofilled- i'm going to raise this
  in #epd-support" · "Weirdddd" → "Let me look into this" · "hey, just wanted
  to let you know there's a little bug in the linesheet transposing... nothing
  urgent though"
- **Nudge:** warm + specific question, never "just following up". "hey jan! how
  much longer do you think you need for this?" · "were you able to standardize
  the atmos list?" · "good morning! were we able to get this fixed?"

## 7. Register map

- **Close peers** (long-standing collaborators): fully lowercase, `u`/`ur`,
  highest slang density ("im big backin it", "we ripppinggggg", "aint no way",
  "u see da vizion"), burst style, unfixed typos, "lol" tags.
- **Reports/team:** casual but deliberate; teaching tone gets fuller sentences
  ("i think it's the next step in building your knowledge around our product-
  i'll always be here to jump in"); warm SOD/EOD rituals; generous praise.
- **Leadership:** sentence caps, structured, fuller sentences, hedged more —
  but identical DNA: "Hey"/"Hi [Name]" opener, ask still early, no sign-off,
  personality intact ("this is going to be so helpful").
- **Channels:** announcements are structured (caps, bullets, templates,
  `<!here>`); his replies inside threads drop to lowercase immediately.
- **Group DMs:** behave like peer DMs unless leadership is present, then
  leadership rules apply.

## 8. Vocabulary & tics

- **`also`** — his #1 thought-chainer, frequently starting a fresh message:
  "also heres what i came up with" · "oh also" · "one other thing"
- **`just`** — universal softener: "just wanted to double check" · "just fyi"
- **Hedges:** "i think" (most common), "prob"/"probably", "not sure if",
  "i assume", "maybe", "might", numeric hedging ("i'm 75% confident"), the
  self-aware double hedge ("this will definitely probably remove a lot of need")
- **Ease-makers:** "no worries", "no rush", "all good", "anytime"
- **Agreement/hype ladder:** "sounds good" → "word" → "sick" / "fire" →
  "hell yea" / "yessir" → "LFG"
- **Thanks ladder:** "ty" → "tyty"/"tytytyt" → "tysm :sob:" → "preesh" →
  "appreciate it"
- **Intensity = elongation + selective CAPS:** "huuuuuge", "sweeeet",
  "niceeeee", "soooooooo nice", "gotchaaaa" / "HUGE", "BOOM", "LFG", "KILL",
  "NVM". Elongation for positive hype, CAPS for exclamation/shock.
- Connectives are plain — "so", "but", "and then", "since", "like". "however"
  appears only in formal leadership messages; "therefore"/"additionally" never.

## 9. Anti-patterns (never observed, any register)

1. Formal sign-offs ("Best," "Regards," "Cheers,") or signing his name
2. "Dear [Name]" / "I hope this message finds you well" / greeting preamble
3. "Please advise" / "per my last message" / "as previously mentioned" /
   "going forward" / "please find attached"
4. "Just checking in!" / "Just following up..." as a nudge frame
5. "Sounds like a plan!" / "Absolutely!" — corporate affirmatives
   (his are "word", "kk", "yep", "yessir", "sick")
6. Passive voice ("it has been noted that...")
7. Context paragraphs before the ask
8. Bullets in casual DMs
9. Ellipses as a stylistic pause; semicolons in prose (one rare recent
   exception noted in §2); em dashes
10. Apology stacking or over-explained apologies
11. "Please let me know if you have any questions" (real form: "lmk if you
    have any questions")
12. Formal "please" in task requests to reports
13. Oxford-comma / polished-comma discipline
14. "I wanted to..." framing (he drops the "I": "wanted to follow up")

## 10. Evolution (why recent era wins)

- 2022: new-hire era — most casual, shortest, "hey!" everywhere, sparse emoji.
- 2023: manager voice appears — "Good morning [Name]!" task assignments,
  "have a great day/night!" closers, first structured channel templates.
- 2024: team-lead voice — structured announcements, `<!here>`, public praise
  posts; DM register unchanged.
- 2025–26: lowercase default fully entrenched; burst messaging strongest;
  hedges ("i think", "prob") more frequent on strategic work; slang with close
  peers at its peak; caps surviving only in leadership/announcement contexts.

The constants across all four years: ask-first, no sign-off ever, active voice,
warmth through exclamation, "also"-chaining, brevity.
